What Students Want and Where They End Up: Aspirations, Local Options and Undermatch in Colombia

Gabriela N. Villalba Marecos · University of Sussex

Session 2
Economics of education, inequality and well-being
Time
Wed 11 Nov, 15:15–15:45
Discussant
Professor Panu Pelkonen

Abstract

Where students enrol in higher education matters for their future academic and labour market opportunities, but their final destinations do not necessarily reveal their aspirations. This distinction is crucial for understanding educational mismatch and the inequalities it reflects.

This paper examines educational mismatch in Colombia, moving beyond conventional institution-level comparisons to study differences across individual programmes, while extending the analysis to students’ aspirations and local educational opportunities. I combine administrative records of secondary-school achievement and university enrolment with aspirations reported before students knew their examination results, alongside information on local educational supply. Mismatch is measured using programme quality indicators based on the academic preparation of entrants and the formal earnings of previous graduates. I also construct a quality benchmark for students’ stated aspirations, allowing me to compare the opportunities they aimed for with those they ultimately obtained.

The results reveal differences that conventional measures of mismatch cannot capture. Among classmates with similar academic preparation, socioeconomic inequalities are already reflected in the quality of the options students aspire to. Disadvantaged students also face greater geographical distances, more limited access to high-quality programmes, and lower rates of realising their stated plans. For gender, the pattern is different. Women and men are equally likely to enrol in their desired field and municipality, but among those who enter a different field, women end up in programmes associated with lower graduate earnings relative to their stated aspirations.

These findings suggest that educational inequalities reflect both aspirations shaped by anticipated constraints and differences in the opportunities students ultimately obtain. Distinguishing these patterns matters for understanding where efforts to reduce educational inequality may need to focus.
