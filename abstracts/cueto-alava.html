Migration Shocks, Institutional Capacity, and the Erosion of Public Trust

Francisco Cueto-Alava · University of Sussex & Universidad Peruana de Ciencias Aplicadas

Session 1
Public economics, taxation and governance
Time
Wed 11 Nov, 12:00–12:30
Discussant
Professor Wenchao Jin

Abstract

Large and sudden migration inflows impose stress on state capacity, yet their effects on institutional trust remain poorly understood in middle-income settings. This paper examines how Venezuelan forced migration affects institutional trust in Peru using a shift-share instrumental variable based on 2007 settlement patterns. I find that a 0.3 percentage point increase in the provincial migrant share raises distrust toward district municipalities, provincial municipalities, and regional governments by 0.85, 0.97, and 0.62 percentage points, respectively.

These effects persist despite only limited measurable labor-market effects and no corresponding increase in measured property crime, while perceptions of insecurity rise, suggesting that the institutional response is not easily explained by material channels alone.

A continuous-exposure specification shows that the increase in distrust is significantly attenuated in provinces with greater historical exposure to migrants. For district and provincial municipalities, the fitted effect remains positive throughout the observed exposure support, indicating attenuation rather than reversal. This pattern is consistent with contact-versus-threat dynamics, while the increase is larger in lower-exposure areas where perceptions are more likely to be shaped through indirect channels. These findings demonstrate that forced displacement shocks can erode institutional legitimacy in fragile governance settings, with implications for migration communication policy and subnational state-building.
