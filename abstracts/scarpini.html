A Light-Touch Educational Intervention to Improve VAT Compliance: Evidence from Eswatini

Celeste Scarpini · University of Sussex

Session 1
Public economics, taxation and governance
Time
Wed 11 Nov, 11:30–12:00
Discussant
Professor Diego De la Fuente

Abstract

Tax administrations across low-income countries are rapidly digitising VAT systems and introducing transaction-level reporting requirements. While these reforms are expected to strengthen monitoring and enforcement, they also increase compliance complexity and generate new opportunities for filing mistakes (Keen & Slemrod, 2017; Waseem, 2025; Lees et al., 2025). This raises an important policy question: can simple and inexpensive taxpayer education interventions help firms navigate increasingly complex VAT systems and improve filing accuracy? Despite substantial research on deterrence, tax morale, and third-party reporting, we know little about whether practical taxpayer education can improve the quality of VAT declarations once digital reporting systems are in place (Mascagni, 2018; Pomeranz, 2015; Naritomi, 2019).

This paper evaluates the impact of a light-touch educational intervention on VAT filing outcomes among VAT-registered firms in Eswatini. The study exploits a randomised survey experiment implemented shortly after a series of major VAT reforms introduced by the Eswatini Revenue Service (ERS). These reforms required taxpayers to move from reporting aggregate figures to submitting transaction-level VAT schedules and substantially expanded the use of digital filing systems. While the reforms generated unprecedented quantities of administrative data, they also created significant challenges for taxpayers and tax administrators (Brockmeyer et al., 2024; Waseem, 2025). Filing errors, inaccurate transaction reporting, and inconsistent schedules have limited the ability of ERS to exploit the full monitoring potential of the new system and have increased administrative burdens for both taxpayers and the tax authority.

The intervention was embedded within a face-to-face survey of VAT taxpayers. Respondents were randomly assigned to one of three groups. A control group received no educational material. A first treatment arm received practical guidance on how to accurately complete VAT schedules and submit VAT returns under the new system. A second treatment arm received an explanation of how ERS uses transaction-level information, how declarations are cross-checked, and how accurate reporting improves the effectiveness of the VAT system. In both cases, information was delivered through a visual infographic accompanied by an audio explanation. Unlike most existing interventions, the messages were delivered by an independent research team rather than through official tax administration communication channels, following an approach similar to Santoro and Mascagni (2022).
