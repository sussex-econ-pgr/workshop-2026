Macroprudential and Monetary Policy under Financial Dualism: A Welfare Analysis for Malawi

Chisomo Faith Kuyenda and Jiao Wang · University of Sussex

Session 3
Macroeconomics, finance and international trade
Time
Wed 11 Nov, 16:30–17:00
Discussant
Professor Andy McKay

Abstract

We investigate the welfare implications of macroprudential and monetary policy in a low-income economy with a large informal financial sector. We develop a closed-economy housing sector-embedded dynamic stochastic general equilibrium (DSGE) model with three households (savers, formal and informal borrowers). The model is calibrated to Malawi, where 33% of households borrow informally, only 1% access the formal mortgage market, and there is a partial monetary policy pass-through to informal lending rates.

We find that the welfare-maximising macroprudential Loan-to-Value (LTV) rule for Malawi is house-price dominant. The rule responds primarily to house price deviations rather than credit growth. This is the case because the formal mortgage market is too thin for credit-responsive tightening to generate meaningful aggregate stabilisation. Reflecting the small mortgage market, the welfare gains from the introduction of an LTV rule are modest when compared with advanced economies.

Moreover, the optimal LTV rule is not Pareto-improving as savers and informal borrowers gain through housing stock stabilisation. Formal borrowers, on the other hand, bear the welfare cost as collateral tightening amplifies their debt volatility without compensating housing welfare gains.

We also find that improving monetary policy pass-through to the informal credit sector generates higher aggregate welfare gains and, unlike the LTV rule, is Pareto-improving as all households benefit. These findings yield concrete policy recommendations for the Malawian authorities on LTV rule calibration, monetary policy transmission reforms, and supply-side structural reforms.
