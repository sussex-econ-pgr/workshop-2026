Bundling Goods and Services in UK Exports: Who Switches and What Do They Gain?

Valeria Terrones · University of Sussex

Session 3
Macroeconomics, finance and international trade
Time
Wed 11 Nov, 16:00–16:30
Discussant
Professor Bridget Kauma

Abstract

In this research, we study why firms that initially export only goods or only services, known as mono-exporters, decide to subsequently export services or goods, respectively, to the same destination and year. Using granular UK data at the firm-destination-year level for the period 2011-2023, we identify which firms have always exported both goods and services to the same destination and year, and which have decided to start exporting bundles (goods and services).

Using a staggered difference-in-difference approach, our preliminary results suggest that only-good exporters that begin bundling services obtain a higher export performance premia than they would have without bundling. We find similar patterns for only-service exporters that begin exporting goods, but these results are not statistically significant. We explore the roles of economies of scope and consumers preference in explaining why mono-exporters decide to export bundles to new international markets, a process known as servitization.
