The Gendered Mental Health Cost of Home Schooling in England

Tomoto Masuda · University of Sussex

Session 2
Economics of education, inequality and well-being
Time
Wed 11 Nov, 14:15–14:45
Discussant
Professor Chirantan Chatterjee

Abstract

This paper measures the mental-health cost of home schooling to parents, using the closure of schools in England during the COVID-19 pandemic, which moved the teaching, supervision and care that schools provide into the homes of most families at the same time. Linking Understanding Society to its COVID-19 Study, I follow the same parents from 2016 to 2024 and place each GHQ-12 reading in its month of interview.

I compare parents in two-parent families whose school-age children were all at primary school with those whose children were all at secondary school: both faced the same closures, but younger children need much more adult help to learn at home.

In a difference-in-differences event study with individual and calendar-month fixed effects, estimated separately by gender, GHQ-12 distress rose during the closures by 1.77 points (0.32 of a standard deviation) more among primary-phase mothers than among secondary-phase mothers. Among fathers the two groups did not differ significantly, and the difference between mothers and fathers is significant at the 5% level in the main specification.
