Does Recentralising Public Education Improve Student Achievement? Evidence from Chile’s New Public Education System

Fernando Rowland · University of Sussex

Session 2
Economics of education, inequality and well-being
Time
Wed 11 Nov, 14:45–15:15
Discussant
Professor Richard Dickens

Abstract

This paper examines whether recentralising the governance of public education improves student achievement. It studies Chile’s New Public Education System (Sistema de Educación Pública, NEP), established by Law No. 21,040 in 2017. The reform progressively transfers the administration of municipal public schools to Local Public Education Services (Servicios Locales de Educación Pública, SLEPs), replacing the previous municipal governance model with a system of public organisations operating across municipal boundaries.

Chile provides a relevant setting to investigate the consequences of institutional change because the reform reverses a governance arrangement introduced during the decentralisation of public education in the 1980s. Its staggered implementation creates variation across territories and over time that can be used to examine changes in educational outcomes following the transfer of administrative responsibilities.

The analysis combines school-level academic achievement data from Chile’s SIMCE assessment system with administrative, socioeconomic, fiscal and political information on municipalities. The analytical dataset contains 67,002 school-year observations covering approximately 8,500 schools, 345 municipalities and 16 regions over the 2013–2024 period.

The main outcomes are average student achievement, mathematics scores and reading scores. The empirical analysis focuses on municipal public schools, whose governance is directly affected by the reform. The staggered implementation of the NEP is used to compare changes in educational outcomes across municipalities entering the new system at different times.
