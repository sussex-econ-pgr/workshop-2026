Collecting Bills and Maintaining the Tax Base: Officer Preferences and Property Records in Punjab

Ahsan Zia Farooqui · University of Sussex

Session 1
Public economics, taxation and governance
Time
Wed 11 Nov, 11:00–11:30
Discussant
Professor Amalavoyal Chari

Abstract

A tax authority can collect every bill it issues and still tax some properties too lightly and others too heavily. I study how frontline officers in Punjab, Pakistan, weigh collecting unpaid bills against assessing the full amount due, and how the property records they administer shape the distribution of tax liabilities. In an adaptive choice experiment with 289 tax officers, I estimate that 60.2% prefer a one-percentage-point reduction in delinquency at the cost of a one-percentage-point increase in underassessment, holding total collections and public support fixed.

Five years of administrative records from Lahore show how discrepancies in the tax base persist. Recorded land area remained unchanged for 92.3% of 21,816 properties. Among 1,927 unchanged records matched to independent field measurements, 45.0% differed substantially from measured land area.

These discrepancies matter for who owes tax. Applying the same statutory schedule to remotely sensed estimates of land and covered area shifts liability towards higher-value properties, raising the Kakwani index of progressivity from 0.042 to 0.090. Mean simulated liability rises by 3.0%, an estimate statistically indistinguishable from zero. A revenue authority that judges performance by collections alone can therefore overlook persistent errors that alter the progressivity of the tax it administers.
